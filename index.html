Planning your visit

The essentials, in one place. Details can differ per edition — the visitor policy below applies specifically to Arcen Castle Gardens.

Re-entry is unlimited

For the 2026 edition you may leave and return to the festival grounds as many times as you like throughout the day. Just be sure to exit via the official exit points.

Children

Elfians aged 0 to 2 enter free. Little Elf tickets cover ages 3–11. Costumes are warmly encouraged at every age.

Accessibility

A dedicated parking area is available for visitors with a physical or mental disability. You must present an officially recognised disabled parking permit — such as the European Disabled Parking Card issued by the Dutch or German government.

Capacity is limited

Arcen has a capped daily capacity. Arriving early on Saturday gives you the calmest entry and the best light for photographs.

Arriving by horse

Horses get a designated care and parking area. A handler must hold the reins when entering at the Kingdom parking entrance, and owners clean up after their horses.

A horse parking ticket costs €1 and includes hay, fresh water and the occasional apple. Only five riders per day can park a horse.

Bag checks

Security may carry out bag checks at entry. They are always conducted politely and respectfully. Glass bottles, glass containers and hard or breakable packaging are prohibited anywhere on the grounds.

Visitor policy

To keep the Kingdom safe, sustainable and enjoyable for everyone. These rules are conditions of the municipal event permit, not preferences. Security staff always have the final say at the gate.

Weapons & props

Policy changed in November 2025. In coordination with the NCTV and police, no metal weapons of any kind are permitted — even decorative, completely blunt, or sewn into a costume so they cannot be drawn.

  • Allowed: prop weapons of soft, non-metal material — plastic, rubber, foam, LARP-safe or other flexible materials — clearly recognisable as props.
  • All props must be completely blunt: no sharp edges, pointed tips or hard impact surfaces.
  • Wooden props only if lightweight, blunt and generally under about 1 metre. Simple staffs within that range are usually fine. Mallets, large hammers or anything over a metre that could cause real impact may be refused.
  • 3D-printed props in plastic, rubber or soft materials are fine. Metal or hard-material prints are prohibited.
  • Never allowed: replica or 3D-printed firearms resembling real guns in size, shape or colour — under Dutch law these count as real weapons. Airsoft and paintball guns are strictly prohibited, including without ammunition or batteries.
  • Weapon use is permitted only for entertainment groups hired by the organisation, under expert supervision.

Everything is inspected at the entrance. If an item is refused you can return it to your vehicle, or hand it to security against a numbered ticket and collect it on your way out.

If you buy a weapon at the festival

It must stay sealed and out of sight on the grounds, the parking, the shuttle and any public area — you may not unwrap it or wear it as part of a costume. When carrying it home it must require three distinct actions before it could be used: sealed inside a closed bag, sheath, box or case; that container additionally secured with a strap, tape, buckle or lock; and a final deliberate action to retrieve it. Police check for this, visibly and invisibly, and you are responsible for compliance with Dutch law.

Animals
  • Allowed: dogs (on a leash), horses, birds, ferrets (on a leash).
  • Not allowed: reptiles, lizards, rabbits and other exotic pets.

Dogs and ferrets must remain leashed at all times, and owners are fully responsible for their animals. See Getting here for horse parking.

Food & drink

Visitors may generally not bring their own food or drink. This keeps Elfia compliant with Dutch event, alcohol and food-safety regulations, and keeps pricing and quality consistent across food partners.

Permitted exceptions:

  • One sealed plastic water bottle, maximum 500 ml.
  • Baby food or infant formula.
  • Food or drink for medical or dietary needs — allergies, diabetes and similar — limited to what is strictly necessary. A few sandwiches, dextrose tablets, fruit juice or a can of cola is fine; full meals or bags of food are not.

You may be asked to show valid medical proof, such as a doctor's note, for those items. Without it they may not be allowed onto the grounds.

Alcohol & drugs
  • Bringing your own alcohol is not permitted, including drinks at or below 15% ABV. Alcohol may only be bought and consumed in designated festival areas.
  • Strong alcoholic beverages and distilled spirits are not allowed on the grounds.
  • Possession or use of drugs is prohibited at all times, cannabis included, however it is consumed. Visitors carrying or using drugs may be removed and denied further access.
Smoking & vaping

Elfia Arcen runs under a municipal event permit from the municipality of Venlo, which makes it a publicly accessible event under Dutch law. Managing health, safety and nuisance is a legal requirement attached to that permit.

  • Cigarettes and vaping — nicotine and non-nicotine alike — only in the designated outdoor smoking areas, which are marked on the festival map.
  • Marijuana is strictly prohibited anywhere on the grounds, including carrying it.

This protects non-smokers and families and keeps food areas, stands and queues smoke-free. The rules are enforced by festival security and local authorities.

Drones

Drones are strictly prohibited for everyone — visitors, photographers and media alike. Flying drones over events and large crowds is forbidden by law.

Political & activist expression

Political, ideological or activist expression is not permitted where it is explicitly or implicitly aimed at influencing, addressing or persuading other visitors, or where it may be perceived as coercive, polarising or disruptive.

Rules can differ from edition to edition. Treat this page as guidance for Arcen; always check the policy for the specific location you are visiting.

About Elfia

Elfia isn't just a festival — it is the world's first outdoor cosplay and costume kingdom. Founded in 2001 as the Elf Fantasy Fair, it took a different path from the start: blending imagination with heritage, and bringing stories to life at castles and historic locations.

Nearly twenty-five years later it continues to grow and reinvent itself. Founded by Stefan Struik, it remains an ever-expanding universe, honouring tradition while constantly introducing new experiences.

It is a kingdom, a community, and an ongoing story. Once you enter, it will capture you.

Where it began

In April 2001 the gates of Archeon opened for the very first Elf Fantasy Fair. Inside the monastery's wood-and-stone hall, Brian Froud, John Howe, Raymond E. Feist, Terry Pratchett and Robert Jordan spoke to an enchanted audience. The Roman quarter held just twenty market stalls of crafts, books and curious treasures.

A fantasy costume catwalk was planned in the prehistoric village but had to be cancelled when a young child dressed as an orc went missing — later turning up at the entrance and giving the medieval soldiers on guard an enormous fright.

A small and quirky start, and the first chapter of the Kingdom of Elfia.
